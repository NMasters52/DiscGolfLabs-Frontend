### Intro to a routine

A pre-shot routine is **a consistent, personalized sequence of mental and physical actions performed before every shot to prepare the mind and body for execution.**

Your pre-shot routine will be specific to your needs and goals. Whether it uses all of your 30 seconds or is only a few.

I encourage the use of pre-shot routines in all facets of your game, but today I will share how to create and leverage your pre-shot routine for putting.

The first step to a putting routine is figuring out how you address your lie currently. Take a second and think, “what do I do after I address my lie?” If the answer is pick my target, check the wind, check the layout of the green… then you’re almost there. The big mistake is all of that is work that can be done before you even address your lie. While you are walking up to your shot from the tee pad or fairway these are the thoughts that should be in your mind and being answered. When you finally step up and address your lie, your 30 seconds should be spent going through a routine. Not mentally figuring out the variables you have had several minutes to observe.

Now we know what to think about before addressing your putt. Let’s talk about what to do before taking the putt. At this point you are 25ft from the basket, You drop your bag, You grab your mini and your putter, and you address the lie. You have all the information we talked about before. What do you do now? You go through the routine!

For me it is the repetition of a familiar motion that quiets the analytical brain. I choose my motions on what gets me to my flow state the fastest. I use a full dry run of my putt to simulate me actually taking the action. This helps when I am mentally nervous, or have been sitting on a backup or I haven’t thrown a putt in several holes. You can shorten this dry run to just your arm motion or just your fingers popping the disc. I wouldn’t add unnecessary or too many motions or ques to start. Start simple, understand why you are adding this to your routine, and drill it.

I encourage you to watch disc golf videos tonight and take note of the players pre-shot routines. See how long it takes, what they do, and put yourself in their shoes. Ask yourself, “why would this player do that weird movement in their routine?”

Next we need to use this pre-shot routine consistently. This isn’t a tournament exclusive. This routine is a lifestyle. It is to be leveraged with every putt. The routine is used to make as many putts as possible. If using it on every putt raises the chances of making it, why wouldn’t you use it every time.

I am going to teach you a personal favorite drill of mine that I use before tournaments and when tweaking my routine. It is called the place and putt. You take your stack of putter and toss them to different distances and with different obstacles. You then step up to each putt with the intention of it being a tournament putt. Go through the routine and execute. This helps build the tournament or league round putting muscle, and the discipline to do the routine every time.

The ultimate goal is for your routine to bring you back to the practice green. Where you jam putts daily. The biggest putting woe you hear is, “I feel I make every putt in my backyard. Then I come out here in a tournament and I can’t even make a 10 ft putt!” They aren’t delusional, they do make all those putts in the backyard. They just can’t translate it to the course. Your routine puts your backyard in your pocket.

During todays drill we will still be doing the same game we have been to track your progress. But this time do your routine before the first putt of each station. If you don’t have a routine do the “place and putt” to iron one out first.

Go practice yours and I’ll see you tomorrow.
