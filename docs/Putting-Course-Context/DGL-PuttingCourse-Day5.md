# Final Lesson: Taking It to the Course

Throughout this course, we've covered a variety of putting styles, techniques, and concepts. But if there's one thing that ties everything together, it's practice.

Before we wrap up, I want to leave you with a few drills that have helped me improve different aspects of my putting game. Hopefully, one or two of them help you work through a weakness or build confidence in an area you've been struggling with.s

## Drill #1: Flattening Your Putt

The first drill is for players whose putt naturally comes out with a lot of hyzer and fades hard to your natural finish.

If you're having trouble keeping your putt on a straighter line, set up a visual obstacle near your typical break point. This could be a tree, the fence, a post, or any object that sits directly in the path where your putt would normally begin to fade. I personally recommend the fence for this drill.

The goal is simple: throw a putt that reaches the basket without hitting that obstacle.

If your normal release angle causes the disc to fade into the object, you'll need to learn how to flatten your release.

Personally, I control my release angle largely through my palm position. If my palm is more downward, the putt comes out on hyzer. If I rotate my palm upward, the putt tends to come out flatter. By exaggerating that feeling during practice, I've been able to develop a straighter putting line.

The obstacle provides immediate feedback. If you hit it, your putt still has too much fade. If you clear it and stay on line, you're making progress.

Sometimes it helps to putt a little harder at first while learning this drill.

## Drill #2: The Pole Drill

This drill is not for everyone.

If you're simply looking to have fun in the backyard and hear chains, this probably isn't the drill for you. This is a practice tool designed specifically for players who want to improve their accuracy in a meaningful way.

Take a pole, broomstick, PVC pipe, or any object that's roughly the height of a basket. Add a small piece of tape or a marker at the height you're aiming for.

Now, instead of putting at a basket, putt directly at that target.

Your goal is to hit the pole as accurately as possible.

There are commercial training aids that serve a similar purpose, but when I was first learning I didn’t have a basket. So, I used a pole. It helped my putting stroke stay on target immensely as I was learning and experimenting with my putt.

What I like about it is that it forces complete focus on the target. There's no basket to catch a slightly off-line putt. There's no chain noise to reward a near miss. You either hit your target or you don't.

Think of it like painting a line up and down the pole with your putter.

It's not the most exciting practice in the world. In fact, it can be frustrating because you won't get much feedback other than whether you hit your target. But I've found that after a session of focused pole practice, my putting often feels noticeably sharper over the next few days.

Again, this isn't a "fun" drill. It's a training drill. But if you're serious about becoming a better putter, it's one of the best tools I've used.
