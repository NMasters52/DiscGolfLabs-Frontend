## Recap of Day 1/ Intro to Day 2

### Spin

- Grip overview. Show the fan grip.
- Grip Control. The thumb and pointer finger are how you control the grip strength, the stability, and the angle.
- Grip Spin. Spin is generated mainly from your fingers on the bottom of the flight plate.
- Release Motion. You start with a closed hand, and end with an open one. Moving the fingers from the flight plate (closed) to an open and extended position (open)
- Release Angles. Release Angles are controlled by the way your hand opens. Consistency comes from the hand repeating that motion. Palm up gives it a flatter release. Palm down gives more hyzer.
- Spin Benefits. More spin equals more distance, more control in the wind, and more consistency.

### The Drill - Self Toss

The goal of this drill is to build muscle memory for the way your hand releases, and to build strength for your wrist and fingers. Also, this drill is a tournament legal way to practice during the round.

You start by getting your grip set up in your hand. You then make your putting motion up above your head. You are tossing the disc above your head, focusing on the release. This can be done on the couch, on the course, on backups, anywhere you have vertical space and a putter. Try and see how far the disc will travel up with just your fingers power.

---

### The Lever

- Your lever is how the disc is delivered. The most common being the pendulum or whip we talked about yesterday.
- The pendulum uses your arm as a way to gain forward momentum while keeping accuracy. Less variables, more consistent, missed putts don’t go as far past the basket, but less at times less power. It allows you to keep your arm straight in line with the pole as you progress through the movement. On average push putters miss high or low more than left or right.
- The whip is using your arm to generate more spin, than just your hand alone. It creates a drag with the putt. With this putt comes more variables, more spin, more distance, at times less consistency, and further comeback putts on average. This putt allows you to have more left or right movement with your arms, granting you power and distance. The most common miss is left or right, as your arm hinges at the elbow and the shoulder.

### Bringing them together

The spin and lever are tightly coupled when it comes to creating the putting motion and release. They must work together to create angles, spin, and timing. The goal today is to work on them individually and together. The “self toss” drill isolates the spin aspect. The next drill combines the two. Before I teach you the drill we need to talk about the timing of the two.

- The last point of contact with the disc when putting is your fingers. This happens whether you have a spin or push putt. The timing starts with your arm pushing forward, then your hand releasing the disc as your arm comes to the point of release.
- The release point is correlated with the aiming point of the putt. I choose the “aim small, miss small” approach. I choose a single chain link and try to make the disc release on exactly that chainlink. Inside of the circle my putt releases at the height of the chainlink chosen and when done right it hits it the exact chainlink I am aiming for. 45-55ft and beyond I am playing for the wind or the drop. I will aim for a higher chainlink and maybe a hair to the right. The goals is for it to drop and fade into the middle. But if I stare at the chainlink, have my hand release at it, and reach for it, it gives me the highest chance to hit the intended area.
- Leave your arm extended! This is the best way to gain confidence during a round, and it helps to self diagnose in practice. Once the disc leaves your hand leave that arm extended until the disc stops moving. Make or miss. When you do this, take note of how your hand finished after release. It may tell you what you’re doing wrong, or reinforce what you’re working on. People often call this “giving the basket a hand shake.”
- Overview. Your arm comes down, it lifts up to create some momentum, your arm reaches the point of release, and your fingers then pop forward to create spin, and you leave your arm extended.

### The Drill

**Building the Release at 10–15 Feet**

- Start close, remove pressure. For the first half focus purely on the hand. Release the disc with just your fingers to get use to it.
- Combine the lever and the hand. Lever initiates, hand releases.
- What to observe: where the hand releases, the angle at release, how it feels
- Then introduce aim small: pick one chain link, try to hit it every time. leave that arm extended until the disc stops moving.
- Goal: discover your preferred release angle and make it repeatable
