The Methodology

- I *c*reated this course as I strongly believe in Reinforcement Learning and Micro Learning creating a more impactful effect on students ability to reproduce the lessons learned. In short you’ll, learn in small doses, then put that learning into practice, and finally we will provide you with statistical feedback.
- If you have ever went down the youtube rabbit hole of tutorials and clinics you have likely felt what is called tutorial hell. Tutorial hell is a cycle of watching videos but never really improving on or internalizing the teachings and applying them.
- We break this cycle by taking this course one day at a time. Watch a day, practice a day, and reflect on that day. This will break you out of the old cycle and into a new cycle of building momentum in your game.
- Putting is like a muscle. You have to work it out to have any gains. But putting with the bad form or without intent is a time sink and leads to frustration and a plateau of gains. The goal in this course is to lead you to adopt a more frequent or daily habit, of putting practice with the right intent.

**IV. The 3 Foundations of Putting Form**

Prerequisite - This course isn’t intended for a certain type of putting form. Putting is extremely personal. In this course the focus is on fundamental truths that apply to all putting forms. This gives you the foundation to create your own style and approach. Starting with the 3 foundations of a putt; spin, power, and lever.

Vocab/definitions to know.

- Less Variables. I speak about variables often in my clinics and lessons. They are in reference to each hinge and lever that is used to propel the disc. In general, less variables = more consistency.
- Loaded Position. Throughout the course I will reference the “loaded position.” The loaded position is when your putting routine reaches a point where the only movement for your lever is forward.

1. Lever
   - Your arm is your lever. It acts as a pendulum or as a whip. Depending on your putting style.
   - Pendulum - I reference the push or “spush” putt when talking about the pendulum motion. When it comes to variables the arm movement is minimalistic. It is much more efficient to keep your disc inline with the basket with this style.
   - Whip - To me the whip directly correlates to the spin putt. You have more variables in this putt. Your arm hinges to create a coiling effect. When you extend through your arm it creates a drag, creating a snap in the release. Your fingers still release the disc and create spin, but most of the spin in this putt is generated by the drag effect. More power less control.
2. Spin
   - In scope of putting a discs spin is generated primarily from your fingers. Your fingers act as a spring board. You create this spin even if it isn’t intentional.
   - Starting with grip. Fan grips give you the best control over generating spin and accuracy for putting. **Show How I hold a putter** the further back you place your fingers on the flight plate, the more leverage they have to generate spin. **Demo your finger pop 😜**
   - A good way to feel this for yourself is to go to the basket about 10 ft away and only use your fingers to make the putt. After about 10 putts you’ll start to see how much spin it generates. This applies to both spin and push putters.

3. Power
   - Power in your putt is generated from your legs and good timing. This applies to both a straddle or a stagger putt.
   - Straddle - For those of you who don’t know what a straddle putt is: it is where you set your feet shoulder width apart, and square your hips with the basket. A straddle putt can be used as your main put as it squares you up with the basket or as a way to get around an obstruction as you are stepping our and away from your lie. The motion is simple . You bend at the knees and hinge at the waste on the way to the loaded position. Then, you drive through your putt. Benefits are you gain a lot of pop, there are less variables, and you are squared with the basket. When you reach the Loading Position you push through the putt with your legs and your arm in sync. This creates some power and accuracy.
   - Stagger - The traditional putting stance in disc golf. In this form you have your right foot forward for righties and left foot forward for lefties. Using the leg in the back to generate power by kicking or pushing off the ground with it. The catch: Timing can make or break the transfer of power from your leg to your extension and release. In short it adds more variables, but they translate to more power. The timing is rather simple though. Once your arm is in the loaded position you sync your back leg and arm. When the leg pushes so does the arm, when the arm pushes so does the leg. This creates a load balance, propelling your disc further than if you just used your arm and fingers. The timing is the key here. With your arm and leg in sync it will create your power, and help keep your balance.

**V. The Putting App**

- Intro to the app
  - Under todays video you will find a button to start the putting game. This game is intended to be played daily after each video.
- How it connects to daily practice
  - This game reinforces daily practice by giving you a way to track your make percentage at different distances, and it reinforces a practice routine.
- Demo
  - I use the “Rule of 2” when practicing any facet of my game. The “Rule of 2” is simple. Pick 2 mental queues to focus on, and apply them to every shot. Drill them into your putt by giving them focus on every putt. I will be focusing on the lever and power timing in this session. You can choose whichever of the 3 you need most work on. Or Break it down further and focus on one at a time. The putting game is not limited to one use per a day. It is a tool to be used and abused. It tracks your makes and misses and helps give feedback on distances you need work on.
  - I’ll Click into the application → input how many putters I have → then demo a practice round. On the side of the screen you will see the putting app input how many putts I made as I play the game. With me putting to the left of the overlay.
